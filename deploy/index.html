A power and sample size calculator for cluster randomised trials

        

          clustertrial.app calculates statistical power, sample size, standard errors and
          minimum detectable effects for cluster randomised trials, including stepped wedge,
          parallel, parallel with baseline, cross-over and staircase designs. Rather than
          selecting a design from a fixed list, you draw the allocation schedule directly on
          a grid of randomised sequences by time periods, so incomplete designs,
          implementation periods and irregular rollouts can all be costed accurately.
        

        

          Power depends on how a trial will be analysed, so the calculator supports ten
          approaches to inference: model-based mixed model inference with normal or
          t reference distributions, Satterthwaite and Kenward–Roger
          small-sample corrections, generalised estimating equations with independence or
          exchangeable working correlation and robust sandwich variances, and classical
          design effect calculations for comparison. Continuous, binary and count outcomes
          are supported, with conditional and marginal estimands distinguished explicitly.
        

        
    
          
  • Four correlation structures: exchangeable, nested exchangeable with a cluster autocorrelation, exponential decay, and exponential function with a lengthscale.
    • 
          
  • Cross-sectional, closed cohort and open cohort sampling, with individual autocorrelation and a replacement rate.
    • 
          
  • Optimal allocation weights for cells and sequences, rendered directly onto the design grid.
    • 
          
  • Up to three designs open at once for side-by-side comparison of power, degrees of freedom, standard error and minimum detectable effect.
    • 
          
  • Power curves and power surfaces exportable as PNG or SVG, and design diagrams exportable as PNG.
    • 
          
  • Sessions saved to and restored from JSON, so a calculation can be revisited when assumptions change.
    • 
          
  • Warnings when the selected estimator is materially more optimistic than the alternatives, and when specified correlation parameters cannot be realised in the fitted model.
    • 
          
  • A verification bundle containing every matrix in the computation plus an R script that independently reproduces the standard error.
    • 
        

        

          Everything runs locally in your browser through a WebAssembly build of the
          statistical engine. No design, parameter or result is transmitted anywhere, and
          there is nothing to install and no account to create.
